## How the year looks in the vineyards

Frascati sits on the slopes of the Alban Hills, about 20 km south-east of Rome, and its wine is mostly white, made largely from Malvasia grapes. The tasting in your glass changes little through the year. What changes is the countryside around the farmhouse: bare or green vines, grapes coming in at harvest, and how pleasant it is to stroll through town before lunch.

**September and early October: the harvest.** The vendemmia usually runs through September, with some picking into early October, and the dates move with the weather. You may pass crates of grapes at the cellar door or crews in the rows, though a standard tour visits the vineyard and cellar rather than taking part in the picking. Days are still warm, and in 2026 the Sagra dell'Uva in nearby Marino ran across the end of September and the start of October.

**Late October and November: falling leaves.** Once the grapes are in, the leaves turn and fall, and the rain arrives. November is the wettest month, so a waterproof layer earns its place in your bag.

**December to February: bare vines and cellar days.** The vines are dormant and the vineyards are bare. For which winter format to book and what to do in rain, see [Frascati wine tours in winter](/guides/frascati-wine-tours-winter/). For the Christmas Village and festive events, see our guide to [Frascati at Christmas 2026](/guides/frascati-christmas/). It is the coolest part of the year, with highs around 12°C at Ciampino, and the day leans on the cellar, the tasting and a long, warm lunch.

**March to May: spring.** The vines wake up in early spring, new leaves appear in April, and by May the hills are fresh and green. May is the most comfortable spring month for the town walk, and Cantine Aperte, the national open-cellar weekend, falls at the end of the month.

**June to August: summer.** The vines are in full leaf and the grapes ripen. June is warm and mostly dry. July and August are seriously hot, with average highs of about 31°C at Ciampino, so the morning town walk and the vineyard visit can be tiring by midday. Around Ferragosto, the 15 August holiday, some estates and restaurants close, and the featured farmhouse tour takes a break of about two weeks.

## Frascati weather by month

Frascati has a Mediterranean climate: hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters, with the wettest weather from October to December. There is no official long-term weather record for the town itself, so the strip above uses Ciampino, about 8 km away and about 190 m lower. Frascati, at roughly 320 m, is usually a little cooler, by about a degree on a typical day.

What that means for a wine day:

- **June to September:** light clothes, sunscreen, a hat and water for the town walk and the vineyard.
- **October and November:** a waterproof layer and shoes with grip, because these are the wetter months.
- **December to March:** warm layers for a cold morning walk; the tasting and lunch are indoors.
- **April and May:** layers, since mornings can be fresh even when the afternoon is warm.

## When is the grape harvest in Frascati?

Frascati's grapes are usually picked in September, with some picking into early October. The dates move every year with the weather, and they vary by grape and by slope. In the hot 2017 season, the Fontana di Papa cooperative near Ariccia started on 28 August, about ten days earlier than the year before, and Frascati producers told ARSIAL, the Lazio agricultural agency, that their cellars would begin in the first ten days of September. In a cooler year, the Malvasia-based whites are more likely to be picked from late September into October.

So the safest window for harvest activity is September, while early October depends on the year. Cannellino di Frascati, the sweet white, is made from late-harvested grapes, so a few rows can still be hanging after the main picking is done.

**Can you join the harvest?** Not on a standard tour. The farmhouse tours on this site visit the vineyard and the old cellar, then sit you down for a tasting and lunch. At harvest time you are more likely to see a busy winery than to pick grapes yourself, so ask your host what is happening that week.

## The Sagra dell'Uva and other wine events

**Sagra dell'Uva, Marino.** Marino, a few kilometres from Frascati, has held its grape festival since 1925, traditionally around the first Sunday of October. Its best-known moment is the *Miracolo delle Fontane*, when the town's fountains flow with wine. The 2026 edition, the 102nd, ran over two weekends, on 26 and 27 September and 3 to 5 October, with the wine fountains on the afternoons of 4 and 5 October. As of 18 November 2026, no 2027 dates were published on the [festival's official site](https://www.sagradelluvamarino.it), so check it before you fix your dates. The festival is a day out in its own right, not part of a Frascati tour, and it brings busy roads, trains and restaurants across the Castelli Romani.

**Cantine Aperte.** The Movimento Turismo del Vino's open-cellar weekend is held at the end of May across Italy, on 30 and 31 May in 2026. Wineries that take part open their doors for tastings and visits; check which Castelli Romani estates are on the list for your year.

**Fiera dei Sapori d'Italia, Frascati.** In 2026 this food and wine fair was held at Villa Torlonia in Frascati from 17 September to 4 October, with more than 100 DOC and DOCG wines on show. It is a regional fair rather than a Frascati wine festival, and its dates change from year to year.

## How busy is Frascati?

Nobody counts winery visitors by month, so the nearest guide is the number of nights spent in tourist accommodation across Lazio, the region around Rome, from Eurostat. In 2024 the region recorded about 5.7 million nights in July, the busiest month, against about 2.6 million in January. September and October were each at about 86 to 87% of July, close to May and June, so harvest season is not a quiet time. November to February were the quietest months, at roughly half to two-thirds of July.

Rome dominates these figures, so they show when travellers come to the region, not how full a tasting room is. 2025 was a Jubilee year with unusually busy winter months, so we use 2024 as the more typical year. In practice, book one to two weeks ahead for harvest-season and spring weekends, and a few days ahead in winter.
