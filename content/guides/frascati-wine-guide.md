Frascati is the white wine of Rome's volcanic Alban Hills, made in five municipalities about 20 km south-east of the city. It is a blend of **at least 70% Malvasia**, either Malvasia di Candia or the local **Malvasia del Lazio** (*Malvasia Puntinata*), and it comes in three legal tiers:

- **Frascati DOC**, the everyday white
- **Frascati Superiore DOCG**, plus an aged **Riserva**
- **Cannellino di Frascati DOCG**, a sweet late-harvest wine

At its best, Frascati is dry, softly floral and savoury, with a slightly salty edge and a gentle bitter-almond finish. It was one of Italy's first DOC wines in 1966, so 2026 is its 60th year as a protected wine. For decades it was Rome's house white, poured by the carafe in trattorias and exported in bulk, and its name suffered. Single-vineyard Riservas have now won Gambero Rosso's top award in two successive guides. If you are visiting from Rome, the easiest way to taste the difference is at a family estate half an hour from the city.

## Frascati wine at a glance

- **Where:** the Alban Hills (Colli Albani), south-east of Rome: Frascati, Grottaferrata, Monte Porzio Catone, and parts of Rome and Montecompatri
- **Grapes:** at least 70% Malvasia (di Candia and/or del Lazio "Puntinata"); up to 30% Bellone, Bombino, Greco, Trebbiano
- **Soil:** volcanic ash and tuff from the extinct Alban Hills volcano
- **Styles:** dry, off-dry, sparkling, aged Riserva and sweet Cannellino
- **Protected since:** DOC 1966; Superiore and Cannellino DOCG 2011
- **Taste:** white flowers, pear, peach, savoury salinity, almond finish
- **Serve:** chilled, around 8–12°C depending on style
- **Drink with:** Roman food such as cacio e pepe, carbonara, fritti and porchetta

## What is Frascati wine?

**Frascati is a protected white wine from the hills just south-east of Rome.** It takes its name from the hill town of Frascati, but the official zone also covers all of Grottaferrata and Monte Porzio Catone, plus parts of the City of Rome and Montecompatri. A ministerial decree first marked out the zone in **1933**.

The rules require vineyards on **volcanic soils**, and the wine must be at least 70% **Malvasia**. Most Frascati is dry, but the same zone also produces off-dry, sparkling, aged and sweet styles.

## What are the different types of Frascati?

There are three denominations, all sharing the same grapes and zone:

| | **Frascati DOC** | **Frascati Superiore DOCG** | **Superiore Riserva DOCG** | **Cannellino di Frascati DOCG** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Style** | Dry (*secco*), off-dry (*abboccato*), medium-sweet (*amabile*) or sparkling (*spumante*) | Dry | Dry, aged | Sweet, late-harvest |
| **Max grape yield** | 14 tonnes/ha | 11 tonnes/ha | 11 tonnes/ha | 11 tonnes/ha |
| **Min alcohol at release** | 11.5% (sparkling 11%) | 12% | 13% | 12.5% (total) |
| **Ageing** | None required | None required | **At least 12 months**, incl. 3 in bottle | Late harvest; partial drying of grapes allowed |
| **Sugar** | Dry to medium-sweet | Dry | Dry | **At least 35 g/litre** |
| **Vintage on label** | Required (except sparkling) | Required | Required | Required |
| **In a word** | Everyday | Serious | Age-worthy | Dessert |

*Source: the official production rules (disciplinari) for Frascati DOC and the Frascati Superiore and Cannellino di Frascati DOCGs. The Ministry of Agriculture publishes the current texts, including any recent amendments.*

### What does DOC and DOCG mean?

- **DOC** (*Denominazione di Origine Controllata*) is Italy's protected designation of origin: a defined zone, permitted grapes, yields and styles.
- **DOCG** (*…e Garantita*) is the stricter top tier, with lower yields, tighter rules and extra checks.

Lazio has only **three DOCG wines**, and **two of them are Frascati's**: Frascati Superiore and Cannellino di Frascati. The third is the red Cesanese del Piglio.

### What is Frascati Superiore?

**Frascati Superiore DOCG is the higher-quality dry Frascati.** It comes from lower yields (11 tonnes per hectare, against 14 for DOC) and riper grapes (at least 12% alcohol). If you only try one Frascati, make it a Superiore.

### What is Frascati Superiore Riserva?

**A Superiore aged for at least 12 months** (counted from 1 November of the harvest year, including at least three months in bottle), with a minimum of 13% alcohol. Riservas are fuller and rounder, with honeyed, nutty notes as they age, and some see a touch of oak. The best single-vineyard Riservas now win Lazio's top white-wine awards.

### What is Cannellino di Frascati?

**Cannellino is Frascati's traditional sweet wine**, made from grapes left late on the vine and sometimes partly dried or lightly touched by noble rot. It must contain at least 35 grams of residual sugar per litre: gently sweet, honeyed and golden, with acidity that keeps it fresh.

It is also **rare**. Italian Wine Central lists only about ten hectares of vineyard for Cannellino (2021 data), so a glass at the cellar door is a treat. Growers traditionally picked the grapes in late October and November, when the cold stopped fermentation and left natural sweetness in the wine.

## What does Frascati taste like?

| Style | Tasting notes | Serve at | Drink within |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frascati DOC (dry)** | Light, lemony, green apple, almond, gentle salinity | 8–10°C | 1–2 years |
| **DOC off-dry / medium-sweet** | Softer, rounder, a touch of sweetness | 8–10°C | Young |
| **Spumante** | Fresh, lively bubbles; aperitif style | 6–8°C | Young |
| **Superiore DOCG** | Riper peach and pear, white flowers, savoury finish | 8–11°C | 1–4 years |
| **Superiore Riserva** | Yellow fruit, honey, almond, sometimes a hint of wood | 10–12°C | 3–10+ years |
| **Cannellino DOCG** | Golden; honey, dried apricot, nuts; sweet but fresh | 8–10°C | 2–10 years |

*Serving temperatures and drinking windows are general guidance.*

The official rules describe Frascati Superiore as ***secco, sapido, morbido, fine, vellutato***: dry, savoury, soft, refined and velvety. The key word is ***sapido***, a savoury, almost salty tang that many producers put down to the potassium-rich volcanic soils.

### Can Frascati age?

**Yes, more than its drink-it-young reputation suggests.** The rules make a Riserva wait at least 12 months before release, and good Superiore Riservas from named estates keep developing honeyed, nutty notes for years. Everyday Frascati DOC is still best within a year or two of the vintage.

## Is Frascati wine good?

**The best Frascati is very good, and plenty of cheap Frascati is not.**

**Why the reputation suffered.** The 1966 DOC allowed high yields, and Frascati became an export commodity. By the 1980s, as *Decanter* later put it, Frascati was dealing with "low prices, low quality and a dented reputation". Cheap, neutral Frascati is still on supermarket shelves.

**Why it deserves another look.** The 2011 reforms created the two DOCGs, cut yields for the top tiers, required volcanic soils and phased out old high-yield pergola vines. Growers have also been replanting the higher-quality **Malvasia del Lazio (Puntinata)**. The results:

- In the **2026** edition of Gambero Rosso's *Vini d'Italia*, two Frascati Superiore Riservas won the guide's top award, **Tre Bicchieri**: **Villa Simone's Vigneto Falconieri 2020** and **Gabriele Magno's Vigneto La Torretta di Valle Marciana 2023**.
- In the **2027** edition, published in October 2026, **Villa Simone's Vigna Filonardi Riserva 2024** won Tre Bicchieri again.

Skip the cheapest Frascati DOC and **trade up to a Superiore DOCG or Riserva** from a named estate. Better still, taste them at the winery: our [comparison of Frascati wine tours](/guides/choose-frascati-wine-tour/) sets the options side by side.

## What grapes are in Frascati?

| Grape | Role | What it brings |
|---|---|---|
| **Malvasia del Lazio (Malvasia Puntinata)** | The quality grape | Yellow fruit, apricot, sage; body and perfume. DNA studies point to a natural cross of Muscat of Alexandria and Schiava Grossa. "Puntinata" means "dotted", after the tiny spots on ripe berries. |
| **Malvasia Bianca di Candia** | The workhorse | More productive and neutral; white flowers, citrus, apple |
| **Trebbiano (Toscano, Giallo), Bombino, Bellone, Greco** | Supporting cast (up to 30%) | Freshness and structure |

Together, the Malvasias must make up **at least 70%** of every Frascati. Producers who favour Puntinata tend to make richer, more aromatic wines, so look for the name on the back label.

## Where is Frascati made, and why does the volcano matter?

**The Frascati zone lies on the northern slopes of the Alban Hills (Colli Albani)**, an ancient volcanic complex south-east of Rome that last erupted tens of thousands of years ago. Most vineyards face west and north-west, towards Rome.

- **Soil:** volcanic ash, tuff and free-draining *pozzolana*, which producers describe as rich in potassium and low in nitrogen
- **Climate:** Mediterranean, with dry summers
- **Autumn:** the ***ottobrate romane***, warm, sunny Roman Octobers that let grapes ripen slowly, sometimes into November

On a visit you see the volcano in the landscape and underground: many wineries age their wine in cool cellars carved into the soft volcanic tufa. Our guide to [what to expect at a Frascati wine tasting](/guides/what-to-expect-frascati-wine-tasting/) walks you through a cellar visit.

## A short history of Frascati wine

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Roman era** | Cato the Elder, from a Tusculan family, wrote rules for growing vines, and Roman aristocrats built country villas in these hills |
| **16th century** | Sante Lancerio, wine steward (*bottigliere*) to Pope Paul III, praises the wine of Frascati, the root of its "wine of the popes" reputation |
| **1787** | Goethe stays in Frascati and writes of being "very happy here, drawing, painting" |
| **1933** | The Frascati production zone is formally marked out |
| **1949** | Eighteen producers found the Consorzio to fight fake Frascati |
| **1966** | **Frascati DOC**, one of Italy's first DOC wines |
| **1980s** | Mass production and export dent its reputation |
| **2011** | **Frascati Superiore and Cannellino become DOCGs**, with stricter rules |
| **2026** | 60th year of the DOC; Frascati Riservas win Gambero Rosso's Tre Bicchieri in the 2026 and 2027 guides |

## What food goes with Frascati?

Frascati is made for Roman food. Its savoury acidity cuts through rich pecorino sauces and fried dishes.

## How to read a Frascati label

| On the label | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Frascati DOC** | Entry level |
| **Frascati Superiore DOCG** + vintage | The stricter, higher-quality tier |
| **Riserva** | At least 12 months' ageing |
| **Vigneto / Vigna [name]** | Single vineyard: usually a good sign |
| ***Secco*** | Dry |
| ***Abboccato*** / ***Amabile*** | Off-dry / medium-sweet |
| **Spumante** | Sparkling |
| **Cannellino di Frascati DOCG** | Sweet |
| **Malvasia Puntinata / Malvasia del Lazio** on back label | A quality-focused blend |

## FAQ

### What is Frascati wine?

Frascati is a white wine from the volcanic Alban Hills south-east of Rome, made mainly from Malvasia grapes. It is produced as Frascati DOC, Frascati Superiore DOCG (including Riserva) and the sweet Cannellino di Frascati DOCG.

### What does Frascati taste like?

Typically dry and fresh, with white flowers, pear, apple and peach, a savoury, slightly salty edge and a gentle bitter-almond finish. Cannellino is sweet and honeyed.

### What grapes are in Frascati?

At least 70% Malvasia (Malvasia Bianca di Candia and/or Malvasia del Lazio, also called Malvasia Puntinata), with up to 30% Bellone, Bombino, Greco and Trebbiano.

### Is Frascati sweet or dry?

Most Frascati is dry. Off-dry and medium-sweet versions exist under the DOC, and Cannellino di Frascati is the sweet late-harvest style.

### Is Frascati a good wine?

The best Frascati Superiore and Riserva wines from quality estates are excellent: two Riservas won Gambero Rosso's Tre Bicchieri in the 2026 guide and another in the 2027 guide. Cheap supermarket Frascati can be simple and neutral, so trade up to a named Superiore.

### What is the difference between Frascati and Frascati Superiore?

Frascati Superiore DOCG is made from lower yields and riper grapes (at least 12% alcohol) under stricter rules than Frascati DOC. It is usually more intense and characterful.

### What is Cannellino di Frascati?

Frascati's traditional sweet wine, made from late-harvested grapes, with at least 35 grams of residual sugar per litre. It became a DOCG in 2011 and is made in small quantities.

### When did Frascati become a DOC?

In 1966, making it one of Italy's first DOC wines. Frascati Superiore and Cannellino became DOCGs in 2011.

### Can Frascati be aged?

Yes. Everyday Frascati is best young, but good Superiore Riservas keep developing honeyed, nutty notes for years.

### What temperature should Frascati be served at?

Chilled: around 8–10°C for everyday Frascati, 8–11°C for Superiore and 10–12°C for Riserva. Serve Cannellino at about 8–10°C.

### What food goes well with Frascati?

Roman classics: cacio e pepe, carbonara, fried artichokes, *supplì*, *saltimbocca*, porchetta and seafood pasta. Cannellino goes with almond or wine biscuits and pecorino.

### Where can I buy Frascati wine?

At the cellar door in Frascati, from Italian wine merchants, some supermarkets, and through wine-search websites that show nearby stockists.
