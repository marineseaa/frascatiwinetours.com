A Frascati wine day can cost a few euros or well over €100 per person, and the difference comes down to what is included. At our 13 October 2026 check, a town cooking class with two wines started at €35, the small-group farmhouse tour with lunch at €84 and a private tasting with a driver from Rome at €139, all per person. The train from Rome costs a couple of euros each way and is usually extra. Taxis from the station and your group size make more difference to the final total than the wine does.

## What's the cheapest way to taste Frascati wine?

**The fraschetta day.** Take the regional train from Termini, walk up into town and buy a porchetta roll. Then sit down in a traditional *fraschetta* with a quarter-litre of house wine. A couple can do the whole day, train included, for less than one ticket on a tour. You drink jug wine without a guide, and you won't see a winery.

**The cheapest guided tasting.** The town cooking class costs €35 and gives you a meal, two wines and a visit to a wine cave in town, with no taxi needed. Some estates also sell a basic three-glass tasting without food; prices are set by each estate, so ask when you book, and plan on the train plus a taxi.

**Best value with lunch.** The farmhouse lunch tour at €84 includes the town walk, the cellar visit, three wines and lunch. Over four hours that works out at about €21 an hour, with the meal included. The Viator day at €90 runs about five hours with a smaller group.

The [guide to Frascati wine tasting with lunch](/guides/frascati-wine-tasting-with-lunch/) covers the lunch options and the porchetta-and-fraschetta route in more detail.

## DIY vs tour: what does a day really cost?

Here is the same day for **two adults**, using the starting prices above:

| Scenario | What you pay for | Total for two | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DIY: train and fraschetta** | Four train tickets, porchetta, house wine | The lowest of the four | No winery, no guide |
| **DIY: train, taxi and a direct tasting** | Trains, a taxi each way, a tasting for two | Depends on the estate and the taxi | You arrange everything; lunch is extra |
| **Small-group tour with lunch** | Two places at €84, plus trains | €168 plus about four train tickets | Lunch and the walk from the station are sorted |
| **Private tasting from Rome** | Two places at €139 | €278 | Door to door; snacks, not lunch |

For couples, the farmhouse tour often costs about the same as a winery day you arrange yourself, once you add a return taxi and a sit-down lunch. Going it alone clearly saves money in two cases: you skip the winery and spend the day at a fraschetta, or you are a group of six or more and the estate offers a group rate.

## What hidden costs should you budget for?

**1. The train ticket.** Every GetYourGuide tour on this page except the private one meets you in Frascati, and the listings say the train is **not included**. A Trenitalia regional ticket costs a couple of euros each way. Rome's city transport tickets (BIT) are not valid as far as Frascati, so buy a regional ticket. If you buy a paper ticket, validate it before you board.

**2. Taxis from the station.** Many wineries sit a short drive outside town. Taxis in the Castelli Romani are usually booked by phone rather than flagged down, so ask for an estimate when you book and arrange your return pickup at the same time. Don't count on ride-hailing apps out here.

**3. Pickup surcharges.** Private tours usually collect from central Rome. If you are staying near the airport or outside the centre, confirm in writing that there is no extra charge.

**4. Lunch wine.** Some lunch tours pour house wine with the meal, some pour from the tasting and some charge extra. Check the "includes" list.

**5. Bottles to take home.** For many visitors this is the biggest extra, and cellar-door prices are modest:

| Wine | Typical bottle price at the cellar door |
|---|---|
| **Frascati DOC** (everyday white) | Single figures to the low teens |
| **Frascati Superiore DOCG** | Low teens to high teens |
| **Frascati Superiore Riserva DOCG** | Mid-teens to low twenties |
| **Cannellino di Frascati DOCG** (sweet, half bottles) | Low to mid twenties per half bottle, the most per litre |

*These bands are indicative and vary by producer, so check at the cellar.*

**6. Shipping.** Some estates ship. Ask for a quote before you buy a case, especially for an address outside the EU, where duties may apply.

**7. Tips and cover charge.** Tipping is optional in Italy and not included in tour prices. Some osterie add a small *coperto* (cover charge) per person, shown on the menu.

## Why does the price change with group size?

Group size is where prices most often catch people out, especially on private tours.

- **Sliding per-person prices:** many estate tastings and private tours charge less per head as the group grows, so a couple can pay noticeably more each than a group of six.
- **"From" prices and a full car:** on private tours the advertised starting price can be the per-head rate for a larger group. Couples pay more per head, and solo travellers pay the most.
- **Minimums:** the €92 Viator private tasting is priced for at least two people, so a solo traveller pays for two.
- **Per-vehicle pricing:** some private full days charge one price for the car, which is expensive for two and good value for six.

> **Tip:** enter your **real date and party size** before you compare prices. The headline number can mislead.

For when a private tour pays off, read the [private Frascati wine tours guide](/private-frascati-wine-tours/).

## Is it cheaper to book directly with the winery?

**Sometimes, but you give up flexibility.** Some estates sell tastings on their own websites, and a direct price can be a little lower than a platform's. For the Minardi farmhouse lunch tour, though, we found the same €84 starting price on a second wine-tour platform as on GetYourGuide. Booking platforms such as GetYourGuide offer:

- **free cancellation up to 24 hours** before the start on the tours on this page
- **reserve now, pay later** on eligible tours
- **reviews from verified bookings** in one place
- one basket for several experiences

Direct bookings can come with **stricter terms**: a deposit, a cancellation window of several days, set visit dates or a minimum group size.

| | Booking platform (GetYourGuide, Viator) | Direct with the winery |
|---|---|---|
| **Price** | Sometimes a little higher | Sometimes a little lower |
| **Cancellation** | Free up to 24 hours before on the tours above | Set by the estate; some ask for a deposit |
| **Payment** | Card; often pay later | Bank transfer, card or deposit |
| **Reviews** | Verified, in one place | Scattered |
| **Exclusive options** | Fewer | Dinners, longer tastings, farm stays |
| **Suits** | Flexible plans and first-timers | Fixed plans and wine enthusiasts |

## How do I book a Frascati wine tasting?

### Through a booking platform

1. Choose your tour and check what's included: train ticket, transfers, number of wines, lunch, wine with lunch.
2. Enter your **date and real group size** to see the true price.
3. Check the **meeting point for your day**, because a few tours move it on Saturdays (see below).
4. Put the **cancellation deadline** in your calendar.

### Directly with a winery

1. **Email or phone ahead.** Frascati wineries work by reservation, and walk-ins rarely work.
2. Give at least a few days' notice. Some estates group visits on set dates.
3. Ask about **minimum numbers**, as many need at least two guests.
4. Confirm the **language**, any **deposit** and the **cancellation terms**.
5. **Book your taxi** from Frascati station for both directions.

### Walk-in options

Fraschette, enoteche and wine bars in Frascati town take walk-ins. **Wineries do not.** A few traditional fraschette still let you bring your own food and buy only the wine. Ask before you unwrap your sandwich.

## How far ahead should I book?

| Season | Book ahead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Spring (April to June)** | 1 to 2 weeks, more for weekends | Mild weather and school holidays |
| **Harvest (September to October)** | 1 to 2 weeks; weekends first | Harvest interest and Marino's grape festival in early October |
| **Summer (July to August)** | A few days to a week | Hot middays; ask about mid-August closures |
| **Winter (November to March)** | A few days | Quieter; some estates run fewer slots |
| **Christmas and public holidays** | As early as you can | Some tours don't run |

**Saturdays need extra care.** The Viator Classic Half-Day and the farmhouse pasta class both give different Saturday instructions via **Tor Vergata station**, while the farmhouse lunch tour still meets at Frascati station. On **Sundays**, check the time of the last train back to Rome before you book a late lunch. Our [Rome to Frascati travel guide](/guides/rome-to-frascati-wine-tour/) covers both stations.

## What happens if my tour is cancelled?

- **Too few people:** small tours and classes can be cancelled for low numbers. Platforms normally refund in full; direct bookings vary, so keep a back-up plan.
- **Rain:** cellar tastings usually run in any weather. **E-bike tours may be cancelled in rain.**
- **Train strikes (*sciopero*):** Italian rail strikes are announced in advance, and a minimum service is guaranteed at commuter hours. Check the Italian Ministry of Transport strike calendar and Trenitalia's Infomobilità page before you travel. If a strike falls on your date, ask the operator about other meeting arrangements, or switch to a private tour with pickup in Rome.
- **Running late:** tours that meet a specific train often can't wait. Aim for the train before.

## Are there discounts?

- **Group pricing:** the biggest saving. Some estates charge less per person for groups of six or more.
- **Children:** some tours offer a child rate; check each listing.
- **Platform offers:** booking sites sometimes show temporary discounts on selected tours.
- **Booking direct:** can be cheaper for the same visit (see above).
- **Gift vouchers:** sold by booking platforms and some wine-tourism sites.

We found **no reliable pattern** of weekday or off-season discounts on Frascati tours.

## What's-included checklist

Before you pay, check these:

- Is the train ticket from Rome included, or do you buy your own?
- Transfers from Frascati station, or pickup in Rome?
- How many wines, and which ones (Superiore DOCG? Cannellino?)
- Food: snacks, a board or a full lunch?
- Wine with lunch included?
- Olive oil tasting?
- Child rate available?
- Saturday meeting point?
- Rain policy?
- Cancellation deadline?
- Price for your **actual** group size?

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a wine tasting in Frascati cost?

At the 13 October 2026 check, guided options on GetYourGuide started at €35 per person for a town cooking class with two wines, €84 for the farmhouse tour with lunch and €139 for a private tasting with a driver from Rome. A glass of house wine at a fraschetta costs a few euros. Prices change with the date and group size, so check the live price before booking.

### What is the cheapest way to do a Frascati wine tour?

Take the regional train from Rome, which costs a couple of euros each way, and have porchetta and house wine at a fraschetta in town. For a guided option on a budget, the €35 town cooking class includes a meal and two wines.

### Is the train ticket included in Frascati wine tours?

Usually not. The farmhouse lunch tour, the farmhouse pasta class and the town cooking class all meet in Frascati and list the train as not included. The private tours pick you up in Rome, and the Viator e-bike tour includes train tickets. Rome's city transport tickets aren't valid to Frascati.

### Do Frascati wineries charge for tastings?

Yes. Wineries charge for guided tastings and normally require advance booking. Fraschette and enoteche in town sell wine by the glass or carafe with no booking.

### Is it cheaper to book directly with the winery?

Sometimes a little. Platforms offer free cancellation up to 24 hours before on the tours on this page and often a pay-later option, while direct bookings can require a deposit or longer cancellation notice.

### Why is the price different for two people than for six?

Many tastings and private tours charge less per person for larger groups, and some private tours price per vehicle or set a minimum of two. Enter your real group size to see the true price.

### How far in advance should I book a Frascati wine tour?

One to two weeks ahead for spring and harvest-season weekends, and a few days ahead in winter. Saturdays and public holidays fill first.

### Can I just turn up at a Frascati winery?

Generally no. Wineries work by reservation. Fraschette and wine bars in town take walk-ins.

### How much does a bottle of Frascati cost at the winery?

Everyday Frascati DOC costs the least, Superiore and Riserva cost more, and the sweet Cannellino, sold in half bottles, costs the most per litre. Prices vary by producer.

### Do I need to tip on a Frascati wine tour?

No. Tipping is optional in Italy and isn't included in tour prices, though a few euros for an outstanding guide or driver is appreciated.
