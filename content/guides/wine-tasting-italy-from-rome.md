If you are staying in Rome and want to taste wine at a real Italian vineyard, Frascati is the easiest and cheapest option: about 30 minutes by regional train from Termini, for a fare of a couple of euros, and you are back in Rome by mid-afternoon. Its wine has been a protected DOC since 1966 and gained two DOCGs in 2011. A coach day to Tuscany or Orvieto takes about 12 hours, with much of it on the road, and is the better choice only if you want famous Tuscan reds or the hill towns and can give up a whole day.

## Why do so few visitors to Italy reach a winery?

Getting there is the hard part. A survey by Movimento Turismo del Vino (MTV) and the research institute Ceseo, presented at Vinitaly in April 2026, found that fewer than 10% of the roughly 104 million foreign visitors who arrived in Italy in 2025 visited a winery. MTV's president named access as the main obstacle: many wineries sit far from a station, and the journey there is complicated without a car.

Frascati avoids most of this. Its wine zone sits on a commuter line from Rome's main station, and several tours collect you at Frascati station. ### Who should still choose Tuscany?

- **Choose Tuscany** if you love big Italian reds (Brunello, Vino Nobile), want the classic Val d'Orcia scenery for photos, and are in Rome long enough to give up a whole day.
- **Choose Frascati** if you are in Rome for a few days, prefer a relaxed pace, want to meet a winemaking family, or want an easy vineyard visit without losing a day of sightseeing.
- **Do both** if you are staying a week or more. The wines, the landscape and the length of the day are all different.

## Why Frascati counts as real Italian wine country

- **It has pedigree.** Frascati became one of Italy's first DOC wines in 1966. Two of Lazio's three DOCGs are Frascati's: Frascati Superiore and the sweet Cannellino di Frascati, both from 2011.
- **It is volcanic.** The vineyards grow on ash and volcanic soils from the ancient Alban Hills volcano, which locals credit for the wine's savoury, mineral character.
- **It is family-run.** Many tour estates are run by families who have farmed here for generations. You will often be poured wine by the people who made it.
- **It has its own sights.** Cellars cut into soft volcanic tufa, some said to date to Roman times. *Fraschette*, the area's old-style wine taverns. Porchetta from nearby Ariccia. Papal villas. Views back across the plain to Rome.
- **It is Rome's own wine.** For centuries, Frascati and its sweet Cannellino were the wines Romans drank.

### What Frascati doesn't offer

- **Famous reds.** Frascati is mainly white wine; local reds exist but are not world-famous.
- **The Tuscan postcard.** No cypress avenues or Renaissance hill towns on the Val d'Orcia scale.
- **An instant reputation.** Frascati spent decades as cheap carafe wine. Taste the [Superiore DOCG and Cannellino](/guides/frascati-wine-guide/) at a family estate and you will notice the difference.
- **Easy hopping between towns without a car.** Frascati itself is simple by train. Linking it with other Castelli Romani towns works best with a driver or tour.

## What can you combine with a Frascati wine tour?

The Castelli Romani hills around Frascati are full of easy add-ons:

- **Castel Gandolfo:** the papal summer palace above volcanic Lake Albano
- **Ariccia:** home of porchetta (IGP), with a Bernini-designed centre
- **Nemi:** a tiny lake village known for wild strawberries
- **Marino:** where the fountains run with wine during the Sagra dell'Uva on the first weekend of October
- **Grottaferrata:** an ancient abbey

These combinations work best on a full-day tour or with a private driver. Our [private Frascati wine tours](/private-frascati-wine-tours/) guide explains when a driver is worth the extra cost.

## How do you get to Frascati from Rome?

- **Train:** regional train from Roma Termini, about 30 minutes; see [Rome to Frascati by train: tickets and the last train back](/guides/rome-to-frascati-by-train/).

## FAQ

### Can you go wine tasting from Rome without a car?

Yes. The easiest option is Frascati, about 30 minutes from Roma Termini by regional train. Many tours meet you at Frascati station and take you to the winery. Orvieto is also reachable by direct train, in about 1 to 1.5 hours.

### What is the closest wine region to Rome?

Frascati and the Castelli Romani hills, about 20 km south-east of the city.

### Is Frascati worth it compared with Tuscany?

For most visitors staying in Rome, yes. It takes half a day instead of a 12-hour coach trip, you spend more of your time tasting than travelling, and you visit family estates. Choose Tuscany if you specifically want Brunello or Vino Nobile and the classic Tuscan landscape.

### How long is a Tuscany wine day trip from Rome?

Usually about 12 hours, with two hours or more of driving each way.

### What is the best wine region to visit in Italy?

That depends on your base. From Rome, Frascati is the easiest. From Florence, Chianti Classico. From Siena, Montalcino and Montepulciano. Barolo and Barbaresco are best from Alba. For the Prosecco hills, base yourself in Venice or Conegliano.

### Can you do a day trip from Rome to Chianti?

It is possible, but it is a long day of around three hours' driving each way. Chianti is much easier to visit from Florence.

### Does Italy have wine regions near Rome besides Frascati?

Yes. Orvieto in Umbria, reachable by train, plus Cesanese country (home of Lazio's only red DOCG) and the Etruscan coast around Cerveteri, both of which need a car or driver.

### When is the best time for a wine trip from Rome?

September and October, the harvest season, are the most atmospheric. Spring brings mild weather and green vineyards.

### Can I taste Italian wine in Rome itself?

Yes. Rome has good wine bars, enoteche and guided tasting rooms. You will not see vineyards, but you can taste wines from across Italy in a single evening.
