A Frascati wine tour is a good winter day out from Rome if you come for the wine, a historic cellar and a long lunch, and less so if you want green vineyards. The vines are dormant from December to February, but several estates host tastings, farmhouse lunches and pasta classes indoors. Frascati is about 30 minutes from Roma Termini by direct regional train. Book a set time with the winery before you travel: not every estate takes visitors every day, and many tours that say "from Rome" start at Frascati station and leave the Rome–Frascati train ticket to you.
<!--more-->

For a first winter visit, choose a guided winery experience with an indoor tasting, a proper lunch or cooking class, and a transfer from Frascati station. If you already know Frascati, a shorter cellar tasting may suit you better. On a wet day, skip any tour whose main draw is cycling or walking through the vineyards.

## What a winter wine tour is like

Picture an old stone cellar, a glass of chilled Frascati Superiore, local olive oil and a board of cheese and cured meat. Outside, the hills around Rome are green-brown. If the weather clears, you may walk between bare rows of vines or look towards the city from a hilltop terrace. If it rains, the best part of the afternoon is already indoors.

Winter is less photogenic than summer. You lose the leafy vineyards and the chance of sitting outside in warm sunshine, and you gain a reason to make the meal and the conversation the centre of the visit.

A winter trip works best when the winery has something substantial to do inside: a cellar tour, a tutored tasting, a paired lunch or a cooking class. It works less well when the tour is mostly cycling, outdoor panoramic tastings or long vineyard walks.

Wine work carries on after the harvest. Grapes are picked earlier in the year, so winter is not the season for picking or watching the harvest. A guide can still explain fermentation, ageing, bottling and the local grapes; ask which stage of production you will see on your date.

## December, January or February: which is best?

### December: a Christmas town and a winery

In December you can pair a winery booking with Frascati's Christmas lights and market. Our [Frascati Christmas 2026 guide](/guides/frascati-christmas/) has the market dates and events. The 2026–27 Christmas Village runs from 21 November 2026 to 10 January 2027, separately from the winery tours.

Choose December if you want the market and lights as well as a tasting, don't mind a busy town centre on popular dates, and can plan around short days.

Watch out for 8 December (Immaculate Conception), 25 December (Christmas), 26 December (St Stephen's Day), 31 December and 1 January. A winery listed on a booking site may still close on these days. Confirm the operating date and your return trains.

### January: a quieter, winery-first trip

January is usually the coldest month, and the vines are dormant. A tasting and a long lunch still make a good break from Rome's museums. The holidays run into early January, and 6 January (Epiphany) is another public holiday in Italy.

Choose January if you care more about a hosted tasting and conversation than vineyard photos, and have a flexible day in Rome.

Watch out for reduced schedules, fewer private departures, restaurant closures, and wineries that open only by appointment.

### February: a couple's outing or a cooking class

February is still winter, but the days are getting longer. A pasta class or a wine-and-lunch tour makes a good shared meal, including around Valentine's Day.

Choose February if you are celebrating, want a food-led day out, or know Rome already and want something different.

Watch out for cold, rain and strong wind on exposed terraces; the vineyards won't look like late-summer photos.

## Winter weather in Frascati: what should you expect?

Expect cool to cold, sometimes wet weather; snow is rare. Frascati sits in the Castelli Romani hills and can feel colder than central Rome, especially outdoors at an exposed estate. Average winter highs are about 10–12°C (50–54°F), with colder mornings and evenings, and average lows of around 4°C (39°F).

Daylight matters as much as temperature. In December and January it is dark by late afternoon, and the days lengthen through February. Don't plan an afternoon vineyard viewpoint as if you had summer evening light.

| Weather | What to do |
|---|---|
| Bright, chilly day | Book a guided tasting with a short vineyard walk and enjoy the views while the light lasts. |
| Light rain | Choose a tour with an indoor tasting room; wear waterproof shoes for wet paths and town streets. |
| Heavy, steady rain | Pick a cellar tasting, pasta class or long restaurant lunch; ask whether the vineyard part is skipped. |
| Strong wind | Skip tours on exposed terraces or bikes; choose a sheltered programme. |
| Very cold evening | Head back to Rome earlier, or check the last trains and a taxi backup before staying for dinner. |

What to wear: layers, a warm jacket, a scarf, closed shoes with grip and a compact umbrella or waterproof shell. Cellars can be cool even when the tasting room is heated. Leave heels and thin-soled shoes in Rome if there is a vineyard walk.

## How to get to a Frascati winery from Rome in winter

### Step 1: Take the train to Frascati

The simplest public-transport route is the regional train from Roma Termini to Frascati, which takes about 30 minutes. Check both journeys on the [Trenitalia website](https://www.trenitalia.com/) or app, because weekend and holiday services vary and engineering works can change the timetable. Our [Rome to Frascati by train](/guides/rome-to-frascati-by-train/) guide covers tickets and platforms.

Don't assume a train runs at the exact time you want. Search both directions for your date, and allow time to reach Termini and find the regional platform. For a timed tasting, arrive early: the next train may be an hour later.

### Step 2: Find the winery's real meeting point

This is where many visitors go wrong:

- "Meet at Frascati station" may mean someone from the winery collects your group and drives you to the estate.
- "Meet at the winery" means the train takes you only as far as town; you arrange the last stretch.
- "From Rome" may only name the city you travel from, not an included hotel transfer or train fare.
- "Pickup available" may mean a paid extra, a fixed meeting point or a transfer on selected departures only.

Minardi includes the local transfer from Frascati station but not the Rome–Frascati train ticket. Merumalia's estate is outside the town centre, a short drive from the station. These are different arrangements, so plan each one on its own terms.

### Step 3: Plan the way back

Visitors often forget the return when they book a late lunch with wine. Check your last convenient train, and ask the winery where and when its return transfer ends. A drop-off in central Frascati may still leave you a walk to the station.

If you plan to drink, don't rent a car and drive back. Take a train-based tour with a station transfer, a private driver or a pre-booked taxi.

With just one day in Rome, count the whole door-to-door time, as well as the tour length. A four-hour winery tour can fill six or seven hours once you add the trains and some spare time.

## Two winter itineraries that work

These are sample schedules with no confirmed departure times. Build your plan around your booking confirmation and that day's train timetable.

### Itinerary A: an easy first winter wine trip (about 6–7 hours)

**9:00–10:30 am: Rome to Frascati.** Allow time to get from your hotel to Termini, take the regional train and reach the winery's meeting time. If the tour includes a town walk, don't book a separate morning walk of the same streets.

**Late morning: Frascati and the winery.** A short walk through town or the vineyard, weather permitting, with more about the architecture and wine history than the bare vines.

**Midday to early afternoon: tasting and farmhouse lunch.** The main part of the day: taste Frascati's white wines, hear how the soil and slopes shape them, try the olive oil and local food, and ask your host questions.

**Mid-afternoon: return transfer and free time.** In the festive season, walk through Frascati's Christmas Village if it is open on your date. Otherwise, stop for coffee or dessert before the station.

**Late afternoon: train to Rome.** Check the exact train before lunch ends. A relaxed return is better than racing for the last useful train.

Who it suits: first-time visitors, couples, food lovers, and anyone who wants a full day out without arranging taxis and restaurant bookings separately.

### Itinerary B: a short indoor tasting plus Frascati on your own (about 4–5 hours or more)

**Late morning: train from Rome.** Arrive with enough time to reach the estate by an arranged transfer or taxi.

**Around midday: a pre-booked tasting of 90 minutes to two hours.** Choose a package with an indoor room for rain or cold, and confirm the length and food.

**Early afternoon: back to Frascati town.** Lunch at a restaurant or a traditional *fraschetta*, depending on opening times and how hungry you are after the tasting.

**Mid-afternoon: back to Rome.** Or stay longer in town if the weather is good and your train allows.

Who it suits: travellers with other plans in Rome that day, or who prefer a tasting to a long hosted lunch.

Avoid trying to visit several outlying wineries on your own in one afternoon without a car or booked transfers. Getting from estate to estate is slow, and winter appointments are fewer.

## What wine will you taste?

Frascati is white-wine country, unlike the red-wine regions of Tuscany. The local wines are Frascati DOC and Frascati Superiore DOCG, usually blends of Malvasia del Lazio (also called Malvasia Puntinata), Malvasia di Candia and Trebbiano, within the denomination rules.

A well-made Frascati is crisp and floral with orchard fruit, and it suits food. You will hear a lot about the volcanic soils of the Alban Hills. They are a big part of the local story, though no wine tastes a certain way just because of volcanic ground. Our [Frascati wine guide](/guides/frascati-wine-guide/) covers the grapes and labels in more depth.

Questions to ask at the tasting:

1. Is this Frascati DOC, Frascati Superiore DOCG or another Lazio wine? The label tells you more than "local white".
2. Which grapes are in the blend? Two wineries can make quite different wines under the same name.
3. Is the wine young and fresh, or aged on its lees or in wood? That changes the texture as much as the flavour.
4. What would you eat with it? Local cheeses, vegetables, roast meat and pasta make more sense discussed with the glass in front of you.
5. Are there other local wines to try? Some estates make reds, often from Cesanese, and the sweet Cannellino di Frascati.

You don't need expert knowledge. Ask your host which wine they open for lunch at home, and which bottle best represents the estate. Those answers often stay with you longer than a technical talk.

## Booking checklist: ten things to check before you pay

1. **Date and availability.** A winery's usual weekly schedule does not confirm 25 December or 1 January.
2. **Meeting point.** Roma Termini, Frascati station, a street in town or the winery itself?
3. **Return transfer.** Where does the tour leave you at the end, and at what time?
4. **Rome train tickets.** Included, excluded, or bundled only with one product?
5. **Indoor plan.** Does the tasting go ahead inside if it rains? Which parts are cancelled?
6. **Meal size.** A cheese-and-salami plate, antipasto, pasta or a full lunch?
7. **Dietary needs.** Confirm vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free and allergy requests before the visit, not on arrival.
8. **Children and non-drinkers.** Can someone join without drinking, and what do they pay?
9. **Mobility.** Historic cellars, slopes and vineyard paths can have stairs or uneven ground. Ask about the whole route as well as the tasting room.
10. **Cancellation rules.** Check the deadline, how bad weather is handled and whether you can change the date.

One extra winter question to send the winery: "If it is cold and raining for our whole booking, what will our group do indoors?" A clear answer tells you more than photos of summer vines.

## Accessibility, children and non-drinkers

Access varies a lot between estates, and even between two tours at one estate. The obstacle may be a cellar staircase, a cobbled courtyard, a steep vineyard slope or the transfer vehicle, as well as the tasting room. Tell the host your mobility needs and ask which parts of the visit are step-free.

For families, a cooking class or a lunch-led tour usually works better than a technical tasting. Minardi lists child prices for its traditional tour and its cooking class. The Freedome listing for Merumalia sets out arrangements for minors and adults who don't drink; check which apply to the way you book.

For non-drinkers, ask whether the tour is still enjoyable without the wine. A good guide, a meal and the setting can make the day for a mixed group; a wine-only tasting probably won't. Check whether water, soft drinks or other alternatives are included.

If you have allergies or dietary restrictions, tell the operator in writing before the day. Last-minute substitutions are harder at a country estate than in a big city restaurant.

## Common winter mistakes

- Booking for the vineyard views alone. Winter vines are mostly bare, so the food and the cellar matter more.
- Assuming every winery opens every day. Many visits are by appointment.
- Reading "from Rome" as "includes hotel pickup". Check the meeting point.
- Following an online itinerary without checking train times. Holiday and weekend timetables can differ.
- Planning a late outdoor visit. It gets dark early, especially in December.
- Taking a tasting board for lunch. Three wines and a snack plate are not a farmhouse meal.
- Skipping the last stretch. Some estates are a short drive, not a short walk, from the station.
- Driving after the tasting. Use the train, a private driver or a taxi.
- Booking a non-refundable outdoor activity in a wet month with no fallback. Ask about weather changes first.

## Who should book a winter Frascati wine tour?

Book one if you want to taste local wine, enjoy a long lunch, like learning from a host more than queueing at another famous sight, and don't mind chilly or wet weather. A cellar tasting or a pasta class makes a good December, January or February day outside Rome.

Skip it, or book a tasting in central Rome, if your sightseeing time is short, you dislike arranging transport, or you want green vineyards or the harvest.

Choose the experience first and the date second. Confirm an indoor tasting, what the meal includes and how you get back; then look at the train timetable. That order saves you a cold afternoon hunting for a winery down an unfamiliar road.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Frascati wine tours available in December?

Some are, with advance booking. December suits cellar tastings, lunches and cooking classes, but the operating days depend on the estate. Christmas and the other public holidays need their own confirmation.

### Do Frascati wineries close for winter?

There is no general winter closure. Some estates advertise tastings all year; others take visits on request or on set days. Ask the winery directly instead of relying on a generic opening-hours listing.

### What is the best winter month for a Frascati wine tour?

December if you want a tasting with the Christmas atmosphere, January for a quieter food-and-wine day, and February for a couple's outing or a cooking class. For green vineyards, choose another season.

### Is a wine tour near Rome worth it in January?

If you book for the wine, the food and a cellar, yes; if you want vineyard photos, no. Choose a short or mostly indoor programme and allow for colder weather and short days.

### Can I do a Frascati wine tour when it's raining?

Often, depending on the tour. The Freedome listing for Merumalia describes an indoor tasting room for winter and rain. Ask whether vineyard walks are shortened and whether the visit moves fully indoors in severe weather.

### Can you get to Frascati from Rome without a car?

Yes. Direct regional trains link Roma Termini and Frascati in about 30 minutes. Reaching the winery itself without a car depends on the operator's station transfer or a taxi you arrange.

### Do I need to book a Frascati winery in advance?

For a hosted tasting, yes. Some estates take bookings on request, and winter availability changes. Don't count on walking in between December and February.

### Is there a winter wine tour with lunch?

Minardi's traditional tour includes a tasting and a chef-prepared farmhouse lunch, plus a town walk and the transfer from Frascati station. It is also sold as the {{< gyg-link tour="410821" >}}Frascati wine tour with farmhouse lunch{{< /gyg-link >}} on GetYourGuide. Check the calendar for your winter date.

### Can I do a Frascati wine tour on Christmas Day?

Only if an operator confirms an opening and a booking for 25 December. A winery that runs tours in December may still close on Christmas Day, and restaurants, trains and taxis need checking too.

### Can I do a wine tasting from Rome in half a day?

With a short tasting, well-timed trains and a winery transfer, yes. A four-hour organised tour plus the return train usually takes more than half a day door to door.

### Is winter cheaper than summer for Frascati wine tasting?

Not necessarily. Winter may bring fewer visitors, but each estate sets its own prices and minimum bookings. Compare what each package includes as well as the headline price.

### Is it better to visit Frascati or Tuscany in winter?

From a base in Rome, Frascati is far simpler for a food-and-wine day: about 30 minutes by train, against a long coach day to Tuscany. Tuscany suits a separate overnight or multi-day trip, especially if you want its particular wine regions.

### What should I wear to a Frascati winery in January?

Warm layers, a weatherproof coat and comfortable shoes with grip. A cellar can be chilly, and an outdoor vineyard walk can be damp even when the tasting room is heated.
