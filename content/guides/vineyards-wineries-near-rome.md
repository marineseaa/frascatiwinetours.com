Rome has vineyards inside its city limits, including a tiny one on the Palatine Hill, but almost none of them take visitors. To taste where the wine is made, head for the Castelli Romani hills about 20 km south-east of the centre. The regional train from Termini reaches Frascati in about 30 minutes, and working wineries are a short taxi ride from the station; a few will collect you. You can taste in cellars cut into volcanic rock, eat lunch on a terrace looking back at the city and be in Rome again for dinner.
<!--more-->

## Wine country near Rome at a glance

- **Closest wine country:** Frascati and the Castelli Romani, about 30 minutes by regional train from Termini
- **Vineyards inside Rome:** real, but private, experimental or open only for events
- **Booking:** almost every winery works **by appointment**, and walk-ins rarely work
- **No car?** Take the train to Frascati or Tor Vergata, then a short taxi or a winery pickup
- **Good months:** late spring, the September to October harvest, and November for new olive oil and the first wines of the year

## Can you visit vineyards in Rome itself?

**Barely.** Rome has vineyards within its municipal boundaries, but they are private, experimental or open only for special events.

| Site | Where | What it is | Can you visit? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vigna Barberini** | Palatine Hill, Colosseum Archaeological Park | A tiny experimental plot of the native Bellone grape, planted as part of an archaeological project | **You can only glimpse it.** Its wine isn't for sale. |
| **Tenuta di Fiorano** | Appia Antica area, south-east Rome | Historic estate of the Boncompagni Ludovisi family, known for one of Italy's early Bordeaux-style reds | Ask the estate; visits aren't routinely offered |
| **Roma DOC farms** | Rome's countryside (the Agro Romano) | Small producers making Rome's own appellation wine | **Contact each producer;** visits aren't routinely advertised |

**Rome has its own wine appellation.** The **Roma DOC** was created in 2011 and covers a broad stretch of central Lazio, from the coast through the Roman countryside to the Alban Hills. Look for it on wine lists in Rome: Malvasia Puntinata whites, reds, rosés and a sparkling style called *Romanella*.

> **Want to taste Lazio wine without leaving the city?** Rome has sommelier-led tasting rooms and wine bars that pour local bottles. For vineyards, head to the hills.

## Where is the closest wine country to Rome?

**Frascati and the Castelli Romani.** These hill towns on the slopes of the extinct Alban Hills volcano (Frascati, Grottaferrata, Monte Porzio Catone, Marino, Colonna and more) have supplied Rome with wine for centuries, and Romans still come up for wine and cooler air.

What sets the area apart:

- **Volcanic soils.** Frascati whites grow on volcanic soil, which gives them a fresh, savoury edge.
- **Cellars in the rock.** Many estates age wine in caves and tunnels carved into soft volcanic tufa, some dating back to Roman times.
- **Views of Rome.** Several terraces look straight across the plain to the city.
- **The train.** It is the one wine area near Rome where a regional train gets you there in about half an hour.

## The best wineries near Rome to visit

Every visit below is **by reservation**. Formats, days and prices change, so confirm with the estate or the booking platform before you go.

### Merumalia: organic wines and a view of Rome

**Where:** Frascati, a few minutes by taxi from the station; the estate can help arrange the transfer.
**What you can do:** a tour and tasting of three organic wines with olive oil, cheese and cured meats, or a longer visit with lunch; dinner is sometimes available. Groups are small.
**Why go:** a family-run, certified-organic estate with a terrace that looks across to the Rome skyline. It also has a few guest rooms if you want to stay over.
**Good to know:** the estate lists a children's rate for its lunch experience.

### Tenuta di Pietra Porzia: a historic cellar

**Where:** the countryside outside Frascati, above the valley of the ancient Lake Regillus.
**What you can do:** guided visits by reservation, with a tasting of at least three wines and bruschetta, typically including a Frascati Superiore DOCG and the sweet Cannellino. Lunch or dinner at the farmhouse can be added.
**Why go:** the cellar connects to tunnels in the tufa that run for around 400 metres and are described as roughly 2,000 years old. The estate also grows Lecinaro, a rare native red grape.

### Casale Marchese: a family estate on Roman foundations

**Where:** Frascati, close to Tor Vergata station; ask whether they can collect you from the station.
**What you can do:** tours and tastings by booking.
**Why go:** the Carletti family's 18th-century farmhouse is built over Roman cisterns, and a stretch of ancient Roman road runs through the courtyard. If you need step-free access, ask when you book.

### Poggio Le Volpi: a winery for lunch

**Where:** Monte Porzio Catone, near the motorway exit; you need a car or taxi.
**What you can do:** eat at the estate's restaurant, which has a terrace facing Rome and a dining room in the barrel cellar. There is also a wine shop.
**Why go:** it is the most restaurant-led winery visit in the area, a good choice when food matters as much as the tasting.

### Principe Pallavicini: an old noble name

**Where:** Colonna and the Marmorelle estate.
**What you can do:** private tastings and estate visits by appointment.
**Why go:** the family say they have made wine here since 1670. Try the sweet Malvasia Puntinata passito.

### Castel de Paolis: for serious wine lovers

**Where:** Grottaferrata; car or taxi.
**What you can do:** guided vineyard and cellar visits with tastings.
**Why go:** barrels age inside an ancient Roman cistern, and a panoramic tasting room looks over Rome towards the coast. The estate is known for its sweet wines as well as its Frascati.

### Antico Casale Minardi: the easiest to book

**Where:** the Frascati countryside; the tours start outside Frascati station.
**What you can do:** the {{< gyg-link tour="410821" >}}Half-Day Frascati Wine Tour with Farmhouse Lunch{{< /gyg-link >}} (from €84, 4.9/5 from 529 reviews) combines a town walk, a cellar tasting and a farmhouse lunch in four hours. The same farmhouse runs the {{< gyg-link tour="601703" >}}Pasta Masterclass & Wine Tour in the Vineyard{{< /gyg-link >}} (from €84, 4.9/5 from 186 reviews), where you make three pasta shapes before lunch.
**Why go:** it is the simplest way to visit a family winery without arranging transport, and the reviews are consistently strong. The train from Rome is not included in either tour.

### Fontana Candida: a historic big name

**Where:** Monte Porzio Catone; car or taxi.
**What you can do:** guided visits on set weekday slots, with a vineyard walk, a tufa grotto and a tasting.
**Why go:** one of Frascati's best-known producers. **Note:** some booking channels offer visits in Italian only, so check the language.

### Gotto d'Oro: a cellar-door shop

**Where:** Marino.
**What you can do:** buy bottles, or **loose wine on tap** to fill your own containers. Guided visits are also bookable.
**Why go:** a large growers' cooperative founded in 1945, and the place for good-value everyday wine.

### More estates to consider

- **Villa Simone** (Monte Porzio Catone): tastings of its single-vineyard Frascati Superiore.
- **De Sanctis** (Frascati area): an organic estate with tours and tastings.
- **Casale Mattia** (Monte Compatri): organic since the 1990s; tastings, picnics and harvest days.
- **Cantina del Tufaio** (Zagarolo): tours of a tufa cave where it ages traditional-method sparkling wine.

**Tips for going car-free:**

- Check the timetable from Termini for both directions, especially the last train back on a Sunday.

## Can you visit several wineries in one day?

**Two is comfortable; three needs a driver.** Without a car, the easiest day is:

1. A morning train to Frascati
2. One winery visit with lunch
3. An afternoon in Frascati town, perhaps with a glass at a fraschetta
4. An early-evening train back

To fit in two or three estates, book a driver or a guided tour. The estates are spread across Frascati, Monte Porzio Catone, Grottaferrata and Colonna on narrow hill roads. The {{< gyg-link tour="848244" >}}Rome Countryside: Wine Tasting Tour with Private Driver{{< /gyg-link >}} (from €139, 4.8/5 from 66 reviews) collects you in Rome for a three-hour vineyard and cellar visit with snacks.

> **Don't drive yourself.** Italy's drink-drive limit is 0.5 g/l, and **zero** for drivers in their first three years and for professional drivers. A few tastings can easily put you over.

## Other wine areas near Rome

If you have a full day or a car, Lazio and neighbouring Umbria have more to offer.

| Area | Travel from Rome | Car-free? | What's special |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frascati and the Castelli Romani** | About 30 min by train | **Yes** | Volcanic whites, tufa cellars, Rome views, fraschette |
| **Orvieto (Umbria)** | About 1 to 1.5 hours by direct train | **Yes**, plus a short taxi | Hilltop tufa city, Etruscan caves, Orvieto Classico |
| **Cerveteri (Etruscan coast)** | Under an hour by train, plus a bus or taxi | Partly | Etruscan necropolis, coastal wines |
| **Cori (Monti Lepini)** | About 1 hour by car | Hard | Native Bellone and Nero Buono di Cori; near the Ninfa gardens |
| **Aprilia (Latina)** | About an hour by car | No | Modern estates growing international grapes on reclaimed land |
| **Cesanese country (Piglio, Olevano Romano)** | 1 to 1.5 hours by car | Impractical | **Cesanese del Piglio**, Lazio's only red DOCG |
| **Montefiascone (Lake Bolsena)** | About 1.5 hours by car | Hard | Est! Est!! Est!!! wine and hand-dug volcanic cellars |
| **Tuscany (Montepulciano, Montalcino)** | 2 to 2.5 hours | Coach tours only | Famous reds, but a long day |

For a half day, Frascati is the easy choice. For a full day by train, Orvieto is the best alternative. Choose Tuscany only if you have a full day and want Tuscan reds in particular.

## Lazio wine in a nutshell

- **Three DOCGs:** Frascati Superiore, Cannellino di Frascati (sweet) and Cesanese del Piglio (red).
- **Many DOCs,** including Frascati, Roma, Castelli Romani, Marino, Colli Albani, Montecompatri-Colonna, Velletri, Cori and Cerveteri.
- **Mostly white:** white wine makes up much of the region's output.
- **Native grapes to look for:** Malvasia del Lazio (Malvasia Puntinata), Bellone and Bombino; for reds, Cesanese and Nero Buono di Cori.
- **Why it's underrated:** for generations Rome bought everything the hills produced, so producers had little reason to raise quality. A newer wave of winemakers now works with native grapes, lower yields and single vineyards, and you can taste the difference.

| **December to March** | Quiet cellar visits and fewer slots |

## Practical tips

- **Book everything.** Almost every estate works by appointment. Some need a few days' notice, and others run visits only on set weekdays.
- **Language:** the main estates that welcome visitors offer English, but some smaller wineries and some booking channels are Italian only. Check when you book.
- **Children:** often welcome, sometimes with a reduced rate, but some estates are adults-only. Ask first.
- **Sunday lunch** is a Castelli Romani ritual, so book winery restaurants well ahead for weekends.
- **Buying wine:** most estates sell at the cellar door. Loose wine is good value if you bring containers. Ask about shipping before you buy a case.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are there vineyards in Rome?

Yes. Rome has vineyards within its city limits, including a small experimental one on the Palatine Hill and historic estates in the countryside towards the Appia Antica. Most aren't open to visitors, so the nearest wine country to visit is Frascati and the Castelli Romani, about 30 minutes away by train.

### What is the closest winery to Rome?

The closest wineries you can realistically visit are in the Castelli Romani hills. Several Frascati estates are a short taxi ride from Frascati station, and some farmhouses near Tor Vergata station are close enough to walk.

### Can I visit a winery near Rome without a car?

Yes. Take the regional train from Termini to Frascati or Tor Vergata (FL4 line), then a short taxi or a winery pickup. The {{< gyg-link tour="410821" >}}farmhouse lunch tour{{< /gyg-link >}} starts at Frascati station, and the {{< gyg-link tour="848244" >}}private-driver tasting{{< /gyg-link >}} collects you in Rome.

### Do I need to book winery visits near Rome?

Yes, almost always. Castelli Romani wineries work by appointment. Wine bars and fraschette in Frascati town take walk-ins.

### What is Roma DOC?

Roma DOC is Rome's own wine appellation, created in 2011. It covers parts of central Lazio including the Roman countryside and the Alban Hills, and includes whites, reds, rosés and a sparkling *Romanella*.

### Which is better for a wine day trip from Rome: Frascati or Tuscany?

Frascati if you want an easy half day, as it is about 30 minutes by train. Tuscany is a full day with long coach transfers, so go only if you want Tuscan reds in particular. For a full day by train, consider Orvieto.

### How many wineries can I visit in a day near Rome?

Two comfortably with a driver or tour; one plus lunch if you travel by train and taxi.

### When is the harvest near Rome?

Usually from September into early October. Marino's grape festival is held in early October.
