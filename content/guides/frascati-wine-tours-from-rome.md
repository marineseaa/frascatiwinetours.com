## Frascati wine tours at a glance

A Frascati wine tour is usually a half-day trip from Rome. You take the regional train or a private car to a family winery in the Alban Hills, about 20 km south-east of the city. You walk the vines, go down into a cellar cut into the rock, taste three local wines with olive oil, and finish with lunch. For a first visit, a small-group tour with lunch covers the most for the money. Couples and groups who want transport door to door can book a private driver, and confident train travellers can do the town on their own.

- **Where:** Frascati, in the Castelli Romani hills, about 20 km south-east of Rome
- **Getting there:** regional train from Roma Termini, about 30 minutes ([Trenitalia, Castelli Romani line](https://www.trenitalia.com/en/connections/castelli-romani-line.html))
- **Typical tour:** about four hours from Frascati station on the {{< gyg-link tour="410821" >}}From Rome: Half-Day Frascati Wine Tour with Farmhouse Lunch{{< /gyg-link >}} (from €84, 4.9/5 from 530 reviews), plus the train each way
- **What you taste:** Frascati Superiore DOCG (dry white), a local red, Cannellino di Frascati DOCG (sweet)
- **Cost:** from a few euros for a do-it-yourself visit to €139 for a private driver; see the [cost guide](/guides/frascati-wine-tour-cost/)
- **Watch out:** on Saturdays, some tours meet at Tor Vergata station, not Frascati; read your confirmation

## Why do a Frascati wine tour?

<img class="hook-image" src="/images/frascati-wine-tours-from-rome-hook.webp" alt="Bottles of local wine and olive oil in a Frascati farmhouse cellar" width="1200" height="630" loading="lazy">

- **It is the closest wine country to Rome.** You step off a city train and reach the vines within the hour.
- **It takes half a day.** Tuscany day trips from Rome mean a long coach ride each way. From Frascati you are back in time for an evening in Trastevere.
- **The wine has history.** Frascati was one of Italy's first DOC wines, approved in 1966. Its two top categories, Frascati Superiore and the sweet Cannellino di Frascati, became DOCG in 2011.
- **The setting is volcanic.** Vineyards grow on the soils of the extinct Alban Hills volcano, and many wineries age wine in cellars cut into soft tufa, some said to date back to Roman times.
- **Rome is on the horizon.** From several winery terraces, and from the terrace below Frascati's Villa Aldobrandini, you look back across the plain to the city.
- **The food matters as much as the wine.** The Castelli Romani are porchetta country and home of the *fraschetta*: a simple wine tavern with paper tablecloths, house wine by the carafe and plates of roast pork.

## What happens on a Frascati wine tour?

Most tours follow a similar order:

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Morning** | Regional train from Roma Termini, on the train your booking names |
| **On arrival** | Your guide or driver meets you outside Frascati station (some tours use Tor Vergata on Saturdays) |
| **Late morning** | Short transfer to the winery; vineyard walk and cellar visit |
| **Around midday** | Tasting of three wines with estate olive oil, bread and local bites |
| **Lunchtime** | Porchetta in town, then a trattoria, osteria or farmhouse lunch |
| **Afternoon** | Back to Frascati station and the train to Rome |

## What will you taste?

| Wine | Style | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| **Frascati DOC** | Dry white | Fresh, easy-drinking everyday white |
| **Frascati Superiore DOCG** | Dry white, higher category (including **Riserva**) | White flowers, pear, a savoury, slightly salty edge and a bitter-almond finish |
| **Cannellino di Frascati DOCG** | Sweet, late-harvest white | Golden, honeyed and dried-fruit flavours; served with wine biscuits |
| **Local red** | Often based on the Cesanese grape | Soft, fruity Lazio red |

Frascati is made mainly from Malvasia grapes: Malvasia di Candia and the local Malvasia del Lazio, known as *Malvasia Puntinata*. Producers who aim for quality tend to favour the Puntinata, which is rarer and gives the wine more body.

For food matches, see our [Frascati wines and food pairings](/guides/frascati-wines-and-food-pairings/) primer.

### Is Frascati wine good?

The best of it is very good, but the name suffered for decades. From the 1970s, mass production sent thin, cheap Frascati to export markets. Since then the 2011 DOCGs, more Malvasia Puntinata, lower yields and single-vineyard Superiore and Riserva bottlings have produced wines you will want to seek out. A tasting at a family winery shows you the best of Frascati where it is made, which the house carafe in a Rome trattoria rarely does.

## How do you get from Rome to Frascati?

Take the regional train from Roma Termini on the Castelli Romani line, Frascati branch.

- **Journey time:** about 30 minutes ([Trenitalia](https://www.trenitalia.com/en/connections/castelli-romani-line.html))
- **Ticket:** a Trenitalia regional ticket. Rome's city transport tickets (BIT) don't cover Frascati. Validate a paper ticket before boarding.
- **Timetable:** check departures and the last train back in the Trenitalia app on the day. Timetables change each December and during engineering works.
- **From the station:** the old town is a short, steep walk uphill, much of it on stairs. Most tours collect you outside the station.

Meeting points, ticket advice and what each tour expects of you on arrival are in our [Rome to Frascati guide](/guides/rome-to-frascati-wine-tour/).

## Many listings, few vineyards

Search for Frascati wine tours and you will find dozens of listings across GetYourGuide, Viator and other platforms. Many lead to the same handful of family wineries. Several "different" tours share the same train, the same driver pickup and the same farmhouse, pouring the same three wines. These are good wineries, so the overlap is no problem, but the tours still differ on:

- **the lunch** (farmhouse, town osteria or snacks only)
- **group size**
- **whether the train ticket is included**
- **the guide and the pace**

**Ask the operator which winery you will visit before you book.**

## Practical tips

- **Don't drive.** Italy's drink-driving limit is 0.5 g/l, and zero for drivers under 21, in their first three years of holding a licence, or driving professionally. Take the train or book a tour with a driver.
- **Wear flat shoes with grip** for vineyard soil, cobbles and cellar steps. Bring a layer: cellars are cool all year.
- **Keep your ticket and the operator's phone or WhatsApp number** to hand.
- **Dietary needs:** vegetarian options are widely available with notice. Request gluten-free or vegan at booking.
- **Children:** many tours accept them, some at reduced prices, but some tastings are for adults only.
- **Tipping:** appreciated, never expected.
- **Buying wine:** wineries sell bottles at the cellar door, and some ship. If you are flying, pack bottles in checked luggage, because hand-luggage liquid limits apply at most airports.

## The do-it-yourself alternative

If you are confident on Italian trains and mainly want the atmosphere, go on your own:

1. Take the regional train from Termini to Frascati.
2. Walk up to **Piazza Marconi** and the terrace below Villa Aldobrandini.
3. Buy a **porchetta** sandwich and take it into a traditional **cantina** or **fraschetta** with a carafe of house wine. That is the old custom, but ask first.
4. Try a *pupazza frascatana*, the town's honey biscuit shaped like a woman with three breasts, "two for milk and one for wine". It is a modern tradition from the post-war years.
5. Check the return train in the Trenitalia app and walk back down to the station.

**What you will miss:** a winemaker, a cellar and a proper tasting. Most wineries need a booking and a taxi, so for those, book a tour.

## A little history

- **Ancient roots:** wine has been made in these hills since antiquity, when Roman aristocrats built country villas around ancient Tusculum, just above Frascati.
- **Papal summers:** Frascati's Renaissance and Baroque villas made the town a summer retreat for the papal court. Villa Aldobrandini was built from 1598 for Cardinal Pietro Aldobrandini, nephew of Pope Clement VIII.
- **The Grand Tour:** travellers to Rome in the 18th and 19th centuries came up to the Tusculan villas for the views and the cooler air.
- **The railway:** the Rome–Frascati line, the first railway in the Papal States, opened in 1856. The same route still takes you to the vineyards.
- **DOC and DOCG:** Frascati became a DOC in 1966; Superiore and Cannellino became DOCGs in 2011.

### How do I get to Frascati from Rome?
By regional train from Roma Termini on the Castelli Romani line. It takes about 30 minutes. Buy a Trenitalia regional ticket, because Rome city transport tickets don't cover Frascati, and check the timetable on the day.

### Is Frascati worth visiting from Rome?
Yes, if you want to see wine country without a long day trip. It is the closest wine area to Rome, with family wineries, volcanic cellars, porchetta and views back to the city, all within half a day.

### Where do Frascati wine tours start?
Most start outside Frascati station, met off a morning train from Termini. On Saturdays, some tours, including the {{< viator-link tour="111408P1" >}}Classic Half-Day tour{{< /viator-link >}} and the {{< gyg-link tour="601703" >}}farmhouse pasta class{{< /gyg-link >}}, meet at Tor Vergata station instead. Private tours collect you in Rome.

### What wine is Frascati known for?
White wine, made mainly from Malvasia grapes on volcanic soil. The top categories are Frascati Superiore DOCG (dry) and Cannellino di Frascati DOCG (sweet).

### Is Frascati wine good?
The best Frascati Superiore and Riserva wines from quality producers are fresh, savoury and full of character. Decades of cheap bulk wine hurt the name, which is why a tasting at a family winery is the best way to try it.

### Do I need to book a Frascati wine tour in advance?
Yes. Book as early as you can, especially for spring and harvest weekends, because small groups fill up. Wineries don't usually accept walk-ins.

### Can I do a Frascati wine tour on a Sunday?
Yes. Tours run on Sundays, but many shops close and you should check the return trains before you go.

### What should I wear on a Frascati wine tour?
Comfortable, flat shoes with grip, and a light layer for the cool cellars. Add sun protection in summer.

### Are Frascati wine tours suitable for children?
Many are, some with reduced child prices. Cooking classes suit families best, while some tastings are adults only.

### Can I drive to Frascati for a wine tasting?
You can, but we don't recommend it. Italy's limit is 0.5 g/l, and zero for drivers under 21, new drivers and professional drivers. Take the train or a tour with a driver.

### When is the grape harvest in Frascati?
Usually from September into early October. Nearby Marino holds its Sagra dell'Uva grape festival in early October.
