For most visitors, the easiest way to pair Frascati wine with lunch is a half-day tour that combines a vineyard and cellar visit, three local wines and a sit-down farmhouse meal. If you prefer to go on your own, several estates near Frascati sell tasting-and-lunch bookings directly. The cheapest version is a porchetta roll and a jug of house wine in a traditional *fraschetta*.
<!--more-->

## What a tasting with lunch includes

Frascati sits in the Alban Hills south-east of Rome, and the regional train from Roma Termini takes about 30 minutes. Many of the estates here are working farms that make wine and olive oil as well as food, and lunch is the part of the day most visitors remember.

Most bookings follow the same order. You see the vines and the cellar, taste three wines, often with olive oil, bread, cheese or cured meat, then sit down to a meal of several courses at the estate or in a local osteria. Expect three to five hours in total, including transfers.

## A typical half-day from Rome

| Time (approx.) | What happens |
|---|---|
| Mid-morning | Regional train from Roma Termini, then meet at Frascati station |
| Late morning | Walk or transfer to the estate; vineyard and cellar visit |
| Around midday | Guided tasting, usually three wines plus extra-virgin olive oil |
| Around 13:00 | Lunch: antipasti, a pasta course and often dessert, with wine |
| Mid-afternoon | Back to the station and the train to Rome |

*Timings vary by operator, so check the itinerary on the listing you choose.*

The three wines usually cover the area's range: a dry white Frascati, a local red, and often Cannellino di Frascati DOCG, the sweet late-harvest white that tends to arrive with dessert.

### 3. Merumalia: organic wines near Frascati

**Where:** Vicolo di Prataporci, Frascati, a short taxi ride from the station.

**What you book:** the estate sells its own tasting-and-meal experiences directly: a tour of the estate, a tasting of its wines with olive oil, then a paired lunch or dinner. The estate describes its wines as organic.

**Good to know:** the estate also has farm-stay rooms. Ask about a taxi from Frascati station when you book.

**Suits:** organic-wine fans, couples, and anyone who wants an evening option.

### 4. Tenuta di Pietra Porzia: a tasting in a tufa cellar

**Where:** Via di Pietra Porzia, in the countryside outside Frascati.

**What you book:** the estate offers a vineyard visit, a tasting inside its cellar cut into the volcanic tufa, and lunch or dinner of traditional Roman dishes at the farmhouse. A shorter tour-and-tasting option without the meal is also on its booking page.

**Why people choose it:**
- The estate says its cellar was dug by hand in Roman times.
- It grows native red grapes alongside its whites.
- It has its own olive groves and presses its own oil.

**Suits:** history lovers and anyone who wants an old cellar as the setting. Book directly with the estate.

### 5. Poggio Le Volpi: a winery restaurant

**Where:** Monte Porzio Catone, a few kilometres from Frascati. You need a car or taxi.

**What you book:** a table at the estate's Epos restaurant, which serves modern Lazio cooking with the estate's own wines. Choose it if lunch is the main event and the tasting comes second. There is a wine shop on site.

**Suits:** food-focused travellers with their own transport arranged.

> **Don't drive yourself.** Italy's drink-driving limit is 0.5 g/l, and zero for drivers under 21 or in their first three years of holding a licence. Three tasting pours and wine with lunch can easily take you over the limit. Book a taxi both ways, or choose a tour with transfers.

### 8. Porchetta and a fraschetta on your own

You don't need a tour for a local Frascati lunch.

1. Take the regional train from Termini and climb the stairs from the station into town.
2. Head to Piazza del Mercato and buy a porchetta panino: slow-roasted, herb-stuffed pork in a crusty roll.
3. Take it into a traditional cantina or fraschetta and order a jug of house wine.

That is the original fraschetta tradition. Winemakers hung a leafy branch (*frasca*) over the door when the new wine was ready, and customers brought their own food to eat at long shared tables. Most fraschette today are full-service osterie with their own menus, though a few still let you bring food. Ask before you unwrap your sandwich.

**Suits:** independent travellers, tight budgets, and anyone who wants to eat as locals do.

## What you will eat

Lunches are usually traditional Lazio and Castelli Romani cooking, served in courses:

- **Antipasti:** bruschetta with estate olive oil, pecorino and other local cheeses, salumi, porchetta, marinated artichokes and seasonal vegetables.
- **Primo:** fresh homemade pasta, often a Roman classic such as cacio e pepe, carbonara or amatriciana, or a seasonal sauce.
- **Secondo** (on longer menus): roast or grilled meat.
- **Dessert:** *ciambelline al vino* (wine biscuits) dipped in Cannellino, or the local pupazza frascatana, a honey biscuit shaped like a woman with three breasts. Local legend says there are "two for milk and one for wine".

Frascati's fresh acidity and slightly salty edge cut through rich pecorino dishes and fatty pork, which is why the local wine and food suit each other so well. Our [Frascati wine and food pairing guide](/guides/frascati-wines-and-food-pairings/) goes through the matches course by course.

## What you will drink

Expect three wines on most tastings, usually:

1. **Frascati Superiore DOCG:** a dry, fresh white with white flowers, pear and apple, and a bitter-almond finish.
2. **A local red**, often an IGT blend or a native grape.
3. **Cannellino di Frascati DOCG:** sweet and golden, made from late-harvested grapes, and a natural partner for dessert.

The Frascati name has had legal protection since 1966, making it one of Italy's earliest DOCs. Superiore and Cannellino became DOCGs in 2011. The wines on your table depend on the estate, so ask your host what you are tasting.

## Is olive oil part of the tasting?

Often, yes. The Frascati hills produce olive oil as well as wine. The pasta masterclass includes a tasting of the farmhouse's extra-virgin olive oil, and Tenuta di Pietra Porzia presses its own. On other bookings, ask whether oil is part of the tasting. In late autumn, look out for the new season's oil (*olio nuovo*), which is grassy, peppery and very fresh.

## Dinner instead of lunch

There are fewer options. Merumalia and Tenuta di Pietra Porzia both offer dinner as well as lunch. Check the time of the last train back to Rome for your date before you book an evening slot, because a missed train means a long taxi ride. If you want to stay late, a farm stay at the estate saves you watching the clock.

## Dietary needs, children and access

- **Vegetarian, vegan and gluten-free:** the pasta-masterclass listing invites these requests at booking. For other experiences, ask when you book and get a clear answer about the planned meal.
- **Allergies:** declare them at booking, not on the day.
- **Children:** many estates welcome families. Check minimum ages on cooking classes and anything involving bikes.
- **Access:** the walk up from Frascati station is mostly stairs, and the old town is steep. The farmhouse lunch tour is listed as unsuitable for wheelchair users, and the town cooking class involves 100 steps. A private driver reduces the travel effort, but ask about steps and surfaces at the winery itself.

## Booking tips

- **Book a few days ahead** in spring and autumn, the busiest seasons; harvest time from September to early October fills up quickly.
- **Check what's included:** the train ticket, the wine with lunch, and whether lunch is standard or an optional extra.
- **Buy a Trenitalia regional ticket** for the train. Rome's city transport (BIT) tickets don't cover Frascati.
- **Note the cancellation window:** often 24 hours on GetYourGuide; direct winery bookings vary.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I do a wine tasting with lunch in Frascati without a tour?
Yes. Merumalia and Tenuta di Pietra Porzia sell tasting-and-lunch experiences directly, and Poggio Le Volpi runs a restaurant. You need to arrange the train and a taxi from Frascati station yourself.

### Is lunch included in Frascati wine tours?
On many, but not all. The farmhouse lunch tour and the pasta masterclass include lunch; the private-driver tasting includes snacks. Read the inclusions on the listing before booking.

### How many wines will I taste?
Usually three: often a Frascati Superiore DOCG, a red and the sweet Cannellino. The town cooking class pours two.

### How long does a Frascati wine and lunch tour take?
The farmhouse lunch tour and the pasta masterclass each run for four hours from Frascati station. Add about 30 minutes each way for the train from Rome, plus time at the station.

### Do Frascati wineries accept walk-ins?
Don't count on it. Most estates expect you to book ahead, even for a simple tasting.

### What is a fraschetta?
A fraschetta is a traditional wine tavern in the Castelli Romani hills. Winemakers originally ran them to sell new wine, and customers brought their own food. Today most serve simple local dishes such as porchetta, salumi, cheese and pasta alongside house wine.

### Can I combine a cooking class with wine tasting in Frascati?
Yes. The farmhouse pasta masterclass includes a vineyard visit and wine, and the town class adds a cellar visit and two wines. In both, you eat what you make.

### Are vegetarians catered for?
Usually, if you tell the operator when you book.

### Is a Frascati wine tasting suitable for children?
Many estates welcome children. Cooking classes are often the easiest format for families.

### Can I drive to a winery lunch?
You can, but we don't recommend it. Italy's limit is 0.5 g/l, zero for new and young drivers, and three tastings plus wine with lunch can take you over it. Use the train and a taxi, or a tour with transfers.
