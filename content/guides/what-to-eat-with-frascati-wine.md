Frascati suits Rome's salty, rich, pecorino-and-guanciale cooking. Pour a **chilled Frascati DOC or Superiore** with **cacio e pepe, gricia, carbonara, fried artichokes, supplì and porchetta**: its fresh acidity cuts through the fat, and its savoury, slightly salty edge matches the cheese. Switch to the local **Cesanese red** for slow-cooked oxtail and lamb. Save the sweet **Cannellino di Frascati** for *ciambelline al vino*, the little wine biscuits Romans dunk straight into the glass.

For generations, Romans climbed into the Frascati hills with a parcel of porchetta under one arm and ate it in a *fraschetta*, a simple wine tavern where the house sold wine and you brought the food. Today the fraschetta usually slices the porchetta for you, and the pairing has not changed.

## Why does Frascati work so well with Roman food?

**Roman cooking is salty, fatty and rich, and Frascati is fresh, savoury and free of tannin.** Three things make the match:

1. **Acidity cuts fat.** Cacio e pepe, carbonara and gricia rely on pecorino, egg yolk and rendered guanciale (cured pork cheek). A crisp white cleans your palate between mouthfuls.
2. **Savoury meets salty.** Frascati grows on volcanic soil and has a gentle, almost salty tang (Italian tasters call it ***sapido***), so it sits happily alongside very salty pecorino romano instead of fighting it.
3. **No tannin, no clash.** Tannin in red wine tends to clash with salt, egg and fried food, making the wine taste harsh. Frascati has none, which is why many Romans drink white with carbonara.

There is a simpler rule too: **what grows together goes together.** Frascati is Rome's own wine, and Romans have drunk it with this food for centuries. If you are new to tasting, our [Frascati wines and food pairings](/guides/frascati-wines-and-food-pairings/) primer shows how to compare a sip before and after a bite.

> **Italian sommeliers call this *contrapposizione***: pairing opposites, so the wine's acidity balances the food's richness. The opposite approach, ***concordanza***, matches like with like, such as sweet wine with sweet food. That is the rule for Cannellino.

## Why are artichokes tricky with wine?

**Artichokes contain cynarin, a compound that can make the next sip of wine taste oddly sweet or metallic.** Rome loves artichokes, so here is how to handle them:

- Choose a **bone-dry, unoaked, fresh** white: a young Frascati DOC is ideal.
- **Fried** artichokes (*alla giudia*) are easier to pair than braised or raw ones.
- Take a **sip of water** between bites and wine to reset your palate.
- Avoid oaky Riserva and red wine with artichokes.

## What is a fraschetta?

**A fraschetta is a traditional wine tavern of the Castelli Romani hills around Frascati.** The name comes from ***frasca***, a leafy branch that winemakers hung outside the door to show their new wine was ready. Customers sat at rough wooden tables, and since the house only sold wine, **they brought their own food**, usually bread, pecorino, salumi and porchetta.

**Today, most fraschette serve full menus.** Expect paper-covered tables, chopping boards of cured meats and cheese, porchetta, Roman pastas, and wine by the carafe or litre. Only a few still let you bring your own food and just order wine.

> **Fraschetta etiquette**
> - **Ask before you unwrap your own food.** Most places now have their own menu.
> - **Carafe wine is generic house wine,** cheap and cheerful but not necessarily Frascati DOC. To taste the appellation, order a labelled bottle of Frascati Superiore.
> - **Order a *tagliere*** (board) of porchetta, salumi and cheese to share.
> - **Expect simple, budget-friendly food** and a lively, local atmosphere.
> - **Sundays are busy.** Sunday lunch in the Castelli is a ritual.

### Where can you find fraschette?

- **Frascati:** fraschette, cantine and porchetta stalls cluster around the old town and **Piazza del Mercato**, where you can buy a porchetta sandwich to take away.
- **Ariccia:** the porchetta capital, with a cluster of fraschette near the town centre and the fullest choice for a fraschetta lunch.
- **Marino, Grottaferrata and the other Castelli towns** have their own.

*Fraschette open, close and change hands regularly. Check opening days before you go: many close one weekday, often Monday.*

## What is porchetta di Ariccia?

**Porchetta di Ariccia is a whole pig, boned, seasoned and slow-roasted until the skin crackles, and it has had protected status (IGP) under EU law since 2011.** It is the signature food of the Castelli Romani and the classic partner for Frascati.

- **Made in:** Ariccia, a hill town a few kilometres from Frascati
- **Seasoning:** salt, black pepper, rosemary and garlic
- **Method:** boned by hand, tied, then roasted for several hours
- **The test:** the crackling should stay crisp
- **How to eat it:** in a crusty bread roll, with nothing else, and a glass of cold Frascati
- **Festival:** Ariccia's **Sagra della Porchetta** in early September (4–6 September in 2026)

## What is the pupazza frascatana?

**The *pupazza frascatana* is Frascati's honey biscuit shaped like a woman with three breasts: "two for milk, and one for wine."**

**The legend:** a wet nurse soothed restless babies with a cloth soaked in Frascati wine. Take it as a local story told with a smile, not as recorded history.

It is made from roughly equal parts flour and honey, sometimes with citrus, and pasticcerie in town sell it. Buy one and eat it with a glass of Cannellino.

## What are ciambelline al vino?

**Ciambelline al vino** (also called *'mbriachelle*, "little drunk ones") are ring-shaped biscuits made with wine, sugar, olive oil and flour, traditionally measured "by the glass" in equal parts. They contain **no eggs or butter**, so they are vegan. At the end of a meal in the Castelli, they come with a glass of wine for dunking. Do it with **Cannellino**.

## What should you eat with Cannellino di Frascati?

**Cannellino is gently sweet**: Italy's rules require at least 35 grams of residual sugar per litre, balanced by fresh acidity. That makes it ideal with **lightly sweet** foods:

- *Ciambelline al vino*, *tozzetti* (almond biscuits), the *pupazza*
- Jam *crostata* and ricotta tarts
- Aged **pecorino romano** or blue cheese, an old local habit
- Dried fruit and nuts
- **Avoid very sugary desserts** and milk chocolate: they make the wine taste thin

> **The dessert-wine rule:** the wine should be **at least as sweet as the dessert**, ideally a little sweeter. Otherwise it tastes sour.

Cannellino is also **rare**: Italian Wine Central lists only about ten hectares of vineyard for it (2021 data). If you find it at a cellar door, buy a bottle.

### Can you eat at a winery?

Yes. Several Frascati-area estates serve food:

- **Farmhouse lunches** come with many half-day wine tours.
- **Winery restaurants**, such as the one at Poggio Le Volpi in Monte Porzio Catone, serve modern Lazio cooking with estate wines and views towards Rome.
- **Tasting boards** of cheese, salumi and olive oil come with most tastings.

## How to pair Frascati at home

### What works
- **Seafood:** grilled white fish, prawns, clams, fish and chips
- **Chicken:** simple roast chicken with lemon and herbs
- **Cheese and salads:** goat's cheese salads, mozzarella, herby green salads
- **Italian classics:** pesto pasta, antipasti, pizza
- **Also try:** sushi, sashimi, Vietnamese spring rolls and mild Thai salads

### What to avoid
- **Very spicy curries**: an off-dry wine copes better with heat
- **Steak, heavy ragù and red-wine braises**: choose Cesanese or another red
- **Sweet desserts** with dry Frascati; with Cannellino, keep the dessert less sweet than the wine
- **Oaky Riserva with artichokes**

### How to serve it
- **Frascati DOC:** fridge-cold, around 8–10°C; drink young
- **Superiore:** around 8–11°C; **Riserva** slightly less cold, around 10–12°C
- **Cannellino:** around 8–10°C, in small glasses

### An easy Roman supper at home

| Course | Food | Wine |
|---|---|---|
| **Aperitivo** | Pizza bianca or focaccia, olives, salami | Frascati Spumante or DOC |
| **Antipasto** | Puntarelle (winter) or raw artichoke salad with lemon and pecorino | Frascati DOC |
| **Primo** | Cacio e pepe or gricia | Frascati Superiore DOCG |
| **Secondo** (optional) | Lamb chops (*scottadito*) or roast pork | Cesanese |
| **Dolce** | *Ciambelline al vino* and a wedge of aged pecorino | Cannellino di Frascati |

## FAQ

### What food goes with Frascati wine?

Roman classics: cacio e pepe, carbonara, gricia, fried artichokes, supplì, porchetta, saltimbocca and seafood pasta. Frascati's fresh acidity cuts through rich, salty, cheesy dishes.

### What wine goes with cacio e pepe?

A chilled Frascati DOC or Superiore. Its acidity cuts through the creamy pecorino and its savoury edge matches the salt. It is the classic Roman pairing.

### What wine goes with carbonara?

A Frascati Superiore DOCG is a classic choice: its freshness balances the egg and guanciale, and it has no tannin to clash with the egg. Carbonara is the most debated pairing in Rome, and some prefer a fuller white.

### Should you drink red or white with Roman pasta?

Mostly white. Rome's pecorino, egg and guanciale pastas tend to clash with tannic reds. A light, low-tannin Cesanese can work with amatriciana.

### What do you eat with Cannellino di Frascati?

*Ciambelline al vino* (wine biscuits), almond biscuits, jam tarts, ricotta desserts and aged pecorino. Keep the dessert less sweet than the wine.

### What is a fraschetta?

A traditional wine tavern in the Castelli Romani hills near Frascati. Customers once brought their own food to eat with the house wine. Today most serve porchetta, cured meats, cheese and Roman pastas, with wine by the carafe.

### Can you bring your own food to a fraschetta?

Only at a few. Most fraschette now have full menus, so always ask first.

### What is porchetta di Ariccia?

A boned, seasoned whole pig, slow-roasted until the skin crackles, from the town of Ariccia near Frascati. It has had EU protected (IGP) status since 2011.

### What is the pupazza frascatana?

A honey biscuit from Frascati shaped like a woman with three breasts, "two for milk, one for wine". Pasticcerie in Frascati sell it.

### What should I eat in Frascati?

Porchetta in a bread roll, a fraschetta board of cured meats and cheese, Roman pasta such as cacio e pepe or carbonara, and *ciambelline al vino* or a *pupazza* biscuit with sweet Cannellino.

### Which wine goes with artichokes?

A bone-dry, unoaked white such as a young Frascati DOC. Artichokes can make wine taste oddly sweet, so avoid oaky or red wines.
